The Magician

The hand behind the wonder

Jadugar Rajtilak belongs to the old school of Indian stage magic — satin and spotlights, doves and disappearances — rebuilt with the pacing of modern cinema. Twelve years and more than five hundred shows later, the mission has not changed: make an entire hall stop breathing at the same instant.

Every evening is engineered for its room. A sangeet gets warmth and participation, a corporate annual day gets precision to the run-sheet, a college fest gets scale the back row can feel. What never changes is the refusal to perform anything the cheapest seat cannot see.

Signature Acts

Three moments the room does not forget

Every Rajtilak evening is built around set-piece illusions, staged with cinematic light and score — these are the three your guests will describe on Monday.

Stage Illusion

The Tilak Revelation

A borrowed ring vanishes in a fist of vermilion dust and returns — sealed inside a tilak-marked locket held by a spectator since the act began.

Mentalism

Fifty-Two Doors

A thought-of card travels backward through the deck, face up, stopping exactly at the number silently counted by the volunteer on stage.
